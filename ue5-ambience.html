Environmental Ambience Design

This project focuses on designing a natural ambience for a game scene using Wwise and Unreal Engine 5. This documentation covers the details of the sound implementation and the specific design choices behind them.

  • UE5
  • Wwise
  • Spatial Audio
  • Sound Design

Project overview

The game operates within a medieval village scene featuring three distinct areas for the player to explore: the church, the village, and open spaces. Each area has its own unique ambience and soundscape.

Audio volumes

The entire game scene is enclosed within the AkSpatialAudioVolume Environment, which provides a quiet overall ambience featuring soft wind. Its child room, Bug Zone, carries cicada sounds throughout. This ambience is looped within a cuboid spatial volume. The sound is set to 2D and converted from stereo to mono to avoid directional cues — if it stayed stereo, bug sounds in one ear would remain fixed even as the player turns, making the environment feel less realistic.

The floor and ceiling of Bug Zone are blocked, while the sides allow its parent ambience to permeate through.

Rooms & portals

The wood house is made up of three separate audio volumes. The one at the entrance has a lower transmittance rate than the main room, letting more of the outside ambience bleed in.

Wwise rooms and portals connect the sounds between spaces, and each room carries its own reverberation rate.

Sound listener

The sound listener is where sound is received rather than emitted. By default, Unreal Engine attaches the listener to the camera — but there is some debate about whether it should follow the player or the camera, as both setups fit different situations. In this third-person setup, the listener is attached to the player (with camera rotation) to avoid sounds abruptly shifting as the camera moves, while still hearing what the player hears at their location.

Reverb

Inside the village it’s important to distinguish exterior spaces from interiors. The two main levers are reverberation rate and sound occlusion. The cleanest way to alter these properties is with auxiliary buses in Wwise using different convolution or plate reverbs, giving each location its own layer of reverb. This project uses reverb buses for small, medium, and large rooms, forests, and the lakeside.

Random sound emitters

A dynamic village soundscape needs randomness, mimicking the way sounds occur in real life. Several sporadic sounds are placed in the scene: insect calls, church bells that chime every ten minutes, and bird calls in the trees. Sound occlusion is turned off for these ambient sounds so they propagate naturally through the scene.

The 3D emitters have circular attenuation areas with definable radii.

Automated sound path

The wild geese are Niagara VFX that fly in circles, so their sound path is set to revolve around the player. While standing under the flock, their calls travel around the stereo field, ear to ear.

Footsteps

To learn which surface the character is on, a ray is shot beneath them — the first material it contacts reports back and switches the material type for Wwise to alter the sounds. The scene uses four main materials: wood, dirt, grass, and stone.

Each sample is sourced from sound libraries and modified to fit the scene. Since the grass here is shallow and soft, a high-pass filter keeps only the high frequencies for a subtle, sizzling effect. Pitch and volume variations add randomness so repeated steps stay natural.

Jump

The jump animation lands each foot at a separate time, so two anim notifies are created: the first triggers a footstep sound, and the second a harder stomp with an additional metal-rustling layer.
